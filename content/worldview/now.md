If I had to describe the world right now in one phrase, I would say it is running hot with less room for error.

Three things are true at once.

**Growth is real but narrow.** AI infrastructure spending of roughly $1.5trn a year drives about a third of US growth. S&P earnings grew 32%. Yet the expansion creates strikingly few jobs.

**Inflation is sticky because its causes are political.** War, fragmentation, chokepoints and capex are all pushing prices up, and central banks only have one tool. On 18 September, the Fed, the Bank of Japan and the ECB all raised rates, the first synchronized hike among the major banks in fifty years.

**The financing is stretched.** Private investors hold a record 73% of federal debt, long yields are above 5%, oil is above $100, and AI credit spreads are widening. Nothing has broken. Everything is tighter.

Dad's framing, as I understand it, is that this is a world where the usual shock absorbers have shrunk. That is the thread running through every note in this section.

## What I am watching next

- **Does the Bank of Japan keep tightening, and what does the yen do?** Dad has pointed out that this one move touches almost every other question. A disorderly yen could spread quickly.
- **Do the bond auctions hold up?** If demand for long-dated Treasuries gets weak, yields could rise further. If it holds, the pressure may ease.
- **Does AI credit hold?** The boom is financed more and more with debt. A big spender cutting back, or a lender getting downgraded, would change the story fast.
- **What happens at Hormuz?** Oil is the fastest way for an old conflict to become a new price shock.

The midterms on 3 November are also on my calendar, mostly because policy rules affect founders and international students.

I do not make forecasts here. I am trying to write down what I would change my mind about. That list is, to me, the most useful part of these Tuesday conversations.
