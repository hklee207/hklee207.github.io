The last arc started with an admission and ended where the whole year began, in the bond market.

## What happened

By the end of May, the US had concluded after eight years that it could not change China. Tariffs gave way to managed trade and roughly $30bn of mutual cuts. Dad's read was that the US had leverage over Japan because it held Japan's security, but never had that over China. The summits were calm and produced little, which worried Tokyo more than a clash would have.

Meanwhile Beijing kept compounding. It ran an industrial policy covering 24 sectors and exported its factories. In July, Moonshot's Kimi K3 took the top open-weight slot, and the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell 10% in a week. Chinese open models reached roughly 60% of US token usage on OpenRouter. Rare earths tightened again, with Japan's dysprosium imports down 82%.

Then yields moved. Inflation would not leave, deficits would not shrink with US debt at $40 trillion, and AI-related corporate bond issuance competed with Treasuries for buyers. The yen sank to 40-year lows near 163, followed by a record 15-trillion-yen intervention and a joint US-Japan operation. The point was so that Tokyo would not have to sell $1.3trn of Treasuries. On 14 September, the US 10-year yield passed 5% for the first time since 2007.

## What Dad emphasized

Dad said the bond market had been the effective check all year. It broke a French government, blocked a tax cut in Japan, and forced an intervention in the yen. It restrained policy when voters could not.

On frontier AI, he made an argument I keep thinking about. Building a top model costs hundreds of millions, the lead lasts about six months, and keeping pace costs a country something like a trillion won a year. His question for Korea was not whether it could compete but whether it should. Open-weight models plus plenty of domestic compute looked like the practical route.

## What I take from it

For a founder, the cost of capital is part of the business plan. When risk-free money pays 5%, every investor will ask why they should not just hold bonds.

For an investor, discipline means choosing the races you enter. Sometimes the winning move is to build on top of what is cheap and open.
