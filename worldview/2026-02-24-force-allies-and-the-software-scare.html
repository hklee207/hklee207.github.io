Allies Stop Trusting, Software Gets Scared

February 24, 2026 · 2 min read

January and February packed in more regime change than any other two months of the year.

What happened

On 3 January, the US struck Venezuela and flew Maduro to New York for trial. Dad pointed out that it was the same calendar date the US seized Noriega in 1990. His bigger point was about consequences. Western governments had spent years telling Beijing and Moscow that force cannot change the status quo, and that argument had just become much harder to make. Oil was widely seen as the motive.

Then the same approach was pointed at friends. Trump threatened tariffs on eight NATO allies over Greenland. Markets slid and gold ran to $5,000. He backed off at Davos, but the lasting mark was in currencies and in planning. Allies now had a reason to reduce their dependence on American weapons, investment and the dollar. Canada's Carney organized the middle powers, and "de-risking" started being used about the US.

In February, the AI story turned on itself. New AI tools that automated legal and financial work triggered a selloff of roughly $1trn in software market value in a week. The panic spread to wealth managers, real-estate services and logistics.

Then on 21 February, the Supreme Court voided most of the emergency tariffs. Within days they were rebuilt as a universal 10%, then 15%, under a declared balance-of-payments crisis that economists said the data did not support. The writer Greg Ip concluded that protectionism is now the settled position of both parties.

What Dad emphasized

Dad's view was that being an American ally had stopped being free. He listed the costs: the Greenland threat, an immigration raid at a Hyundai plant, large investment asks of Seoul and Tokyo. He also argued that the software panic was partly a story about speed, because markets repriced a whole industry on the fear of a capability rather than on lost revenue.

What I take from it

As a founder who builds with AI, I find the software scare sobering. A product that is only a thin layer over something a model can do is exposed.

As an investor, I take away two things. Trust is an asset with a balance sheet, and it can be spent. And when a court strikes something down, assume the policy will return under a new name.
