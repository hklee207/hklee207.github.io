China Answers With Chokepoints

November 25, 2025 · 2 min read

This was the arc where the rest of the year started to make sense to me.

What happened

On 9 October, China did not raise tariffs. It announced export controls on rare earths that reach any product containing even 0.1% Chinese content. That rule follows the product around the world. It rests on decades of state-directed investment that left China with about 90% of refined supply.

Within days, the Dutch government took over Nexperia, China answered with an export ban, and the threat spread to car production at BMW, Toyota and Mercedes. A small chip supplier nearly stopped some of the biggest factories on earth.

At Busan on 30 October, Trump called the resulting one-year truce a "12 out of 10." Most other readers saw something different: Washington accepted a link between its chip controls and Beijing's rare-earth controls, and dropped a threatened 100% tariff to get there.

Gold crossed $4,000 on 7 October, up more than 50% in a year. The name for that flight was the "debasement trade," a bet against paper money, against a Fed cutting while inflation stays above target, and against deficits that do not shrink.

By late November, a second piece became clear. As much as half of US growth in the first half of the year came from corporate AI investment. The boom was no longer just a stock story. It was the growth story.

What Dad emphasized

His point was blunt. A tariff taxes a market. A chokepoint stops a factory. Those are not the same weapon, and the second is stronger. He also pointed out that about 85% of global trade already routes around America, which is why tariffs alone could never do the job.

What I take from it

For a founder, the lesson is to map your supply chain down to the unglamorous parts. The scary dependency is rarely the famous one. It is often a small component or material that one country dominates.

For an investor, the lesson is that when one sector carries a whole economy, the question to ask is what happens if it slows. I did not have an answer yet, and that is why I kept reading.
