Worldview

Every Tuesday since late August 2025, my dad sends me a report on what happened in the world and the economy that week. It is written in Korean, it is long, and it is opinionated. In between, he sends shorter notes on the markets almost every day.

I read them, and then we talk. Sometimes I agree. Often I push back. Occasionally I just ask what a word means and end up learning a whole subject.

I do this for a simple reason. I want to found a company, and later a venture firm. Both jobs come down to the same skill: seeing which forces are actually moving things, and which are noise. A year of weekly reports is a surprisingly good training set. You stop reading each headline as its own story and start seeing one long argument playing out.

The notes in this section are my attempt to write that argument down, one arc at a time. They are my reading of the conversations, not a transcript. Dad's views are paraphrased, and the mistakes are mine.

Lenses I keep coming back to

  • Chokepoints beat tariffs. A tariff taxes a market that people can route around. A chokepoint stops a factory. The second is the weapon that matters now.
  • The bond market is the last adult in the room. Governments argue, and voters get ignored. Long-dated debt has been the one thing that actually says no.
  • The AI boom is real, and so is the thin job market. Spending is carrying growth, but it is not creating many seats. Both facts have to sit together.
  • Being a US ally stopped being free. Trust now has a price, and middle powers are quietly hedging.
  • Ask whether a race is worth entering. Being able to compete is different from it being wise to. That one applies to startups too.

The full archive, with every weekly report, a timeline and map of how the world changed, and the daily notes, is in Tinkerly, the newspaper I built from them.
