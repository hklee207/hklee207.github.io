America Gets Hooked on AI Spending

January 20, 2026 · 2 min read

If the previous arc showed that AI spending mattered, this one showed what it cost.

What happened

The numbers were uncomfortable. Some analysts argued that a fade in AI investment would mean a 20-30% stock drawdown and a real hit to GDP, and that without it the economy might already be in recession. Financing got creative too. Meta moved about $30bn of data-center debt off its balance sheet, and Alphabet issued a bond running 50 years.

The bill arrived in the labor market. October job-cut announcements were the highest for that month in 20 years. Companies that had been hoarding workers, such as Amazon, UPS, Target and Meta, stopped. Unemployment reached 4.6% by December.

Dad kept putting two charts side by side. Stocks up about 70% over three years. Job openings down about 30%. That gap, more than any single policy, is what drove the politics of the winter. Democrats won the November elections on "affordability." Trump answered with a proposed $2,000 tariff rebate. By January the White House had moved almost entirely to that theme, with credit-card caps, mortgage purchases and promises of cash. Fiscal, monetary and credit policy were all pointing the same way for the first time in a while.

What Dad emphasized

He did not want me to treat the AI boom as either a fraud or a miracle. It was real investment producing real growth, and also a very narrow kind of growth. He wanted me to hold both facts at once, and to notice that politicians respond to what voters feel at the grocery store, not to GDP.

What I take from it

For a founder, this is the arc that hit closest. Entry-level roles are exactly where my classmates and I are headed, and a company that creates value without adding many seats is now the normal case, not the exception.

For an investor, it is a reminder that a headline number and a lived experience can diverge for a long time. When they do, politics tends to fill the gap, and politics changes the rules that businesses operate under.

I try to ask of every startup idea: who gets hired, who gets squeezed, and who is paying?
