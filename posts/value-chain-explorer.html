Value Chain Explorer

Type one company name. Get its mapped value chain, its highest-impact recent news, and the experts positioned to speak to each story — with search-ready keywords already written.

July 2026 Live internal tool Built solo · LLM-powered research app English & Korean

What it is

Value Chain Explorer is a research tool I built for business-development prep in the expert-network industry. An associate types an anchor company — a prospect, a client, anything relevant to a mandate — and the tool reads recent news and public sources, works out who that company buys from, sells to, and depends on internally, and lays it out as an interactive map. Every news signal it finds is scored by how much it actually matters to that company and pinned to the exact part of the chain it affects.

The output is not just a map. Each signal arrives with the relevant experts already matched to it, and their database search filters pre-written — company, title, industry, function, region — so an associate can go from "what's happening with this company" to a shortlist of experts to source in one motion.

Suppliers · customers · leadership & strategy — mapped from one search

How it works

01
Search
Reads recent news and filings on the company, filtering out routine PR for developments that actually move the business.
02
Map
Places each finding on the chain: who supplies the company, who buys from it, and what's moving inside its own leadership and strategy.
03
Score
Ranks every signal by how hard it actually hits the company — revenue, cost, or risk — highest impact first.
04
Match
Attaches the experts suited to each signal, with database search terms already drafted and ready to paste.

Why it matters

  • Cuts the manual cross-referencing out of prep
    What used to mean separately checking recent news, sketching a supply chain by hand, and then searching an expert database by guesswork now happens in one pass.
  • Surfaces experts you wouldn't have thought to search for
    Because the map goes a layer deeper than the obvious players — a chipmaker's equipment supplier, that supplier's biggest customer — it turns up sourcing angles a first-pass search alone would miss.
  • Hands you the client-ready line, not just the data
    Every signal comes with a plain-language reason it matters to that company — the exact framing an associate can repeat straight into a client conversation.
  • Runs in Korean as well as English
    Every label and every generated description can be switched to Korean on demand, so it's usable directly inside a Korean-language client engagement, not just as a translation step afterward.
  • Any node becomes its own starting point
    Click a supplier or customer inside the map and re-center the whole tool on it — useful the moment a conversation shifts from the prospect to a name that came up inside its chain.
